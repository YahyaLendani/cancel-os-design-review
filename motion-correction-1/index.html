Design review: motion correction 1

A small motion correction on the website and the Android app, on top of the polish-1 checkpoint 49b86de. Layout, palette and identity are unchanged.

  • Website: the whole-page fade-and-drift on load and navigation is removed, with nothing in its place. Server-rendered content is no longer hidden while the page's scripts load.
  • Website: the dashboard cards that show amounts no longer drift in.
  • Website: a pressed button now settles to 0.98, the same as the phone (it was 0.97).
  • Android: the header's More options menu can be reopened while it fades out.
  • Kept: button, menu and navigation feedback; the loader for data that is genuinely pending, with its curve unchanged; every reduced-motion path.

Every screen uses synthetic test data (account wizard@example.test, services named "Synthetic …") on a local stand-in server. No real person's data appears.

Motion approval is pending your review of these recordings. Screen-reader behaviour, smoothness on a physical device and release readiness are unverified.

Android: reopening the header menu during its fade

The emulator's own screen recording, at real speed. Light, animations on. No captions in the frame. The moments, from the recording logs:

  1. About 1.6 s: More options. The menu opens.
  2. About 3.2 s: Back, then More options again. From the device's input log, the tap came 84 ms (before) and 82 ms (after) after Back, inside the menu's 120 ms fade.
  3. About 7.5 s: Back. The menu fades out.

Whether the menu was open after the reopen was read from the app's UI tree (a uiautomator dump: the menu's items present or not): before, false; after, true.

Results

Website (check:web-motion, a new check)

  • Before: 15 checks failed. With scripts held or disabled, content was at opacity 0. After navigation, 168–180 frames were faded or shifted. The dashboard's amount cards moved. A press reached 0.9697.
  • After, b85e0cf: all passed. Content was visible with scripts held and with JavaScript disabled. Navigation had 0 faded or shifted frames, with motion on and under reduced motion. The amount cards had 0 moving frames. A press reached 0.98. The row menu still grows, and the loader still moves (and stands still under reduced motion).
  • Keyboard focus (check:edit-first-click): 63 of 63 passed on b85e0cf, including the 390 px focus-visibility correction.

What this does and doesn't show. The whole-page animation no longer hides server-rendered content. That doesn't make every page work without JavaScript: /subscriptions still shows only its loader with scripts disabled, because its rows stream in and need script to appear.

Android (check:android-menu-reopen, a new check, on the emulator)

Each attempt is Back, then a tap on More options. The delay between them was read from the device's input log. Every case ran with animations on and again under Remove animations.

Both modes—A tap outside the menu (on a row) closes it without opening the row; Back closes it and stays on the list; More options while open closes it. Passed before and after.
  • Fixed in the tested emulator configuration: reopening during the fade. Before, the menu's full-screen backdrop covered the button while it faded and a tap there closed the menu. Now a tap on the button's area while closing reopens it.
  • Unresolved: the window-removal gap. After the menu goes, Android removes its window about 50 ms later than the app removes the backdrop. A tap in between can be lost: after the fix, 4 of the 5 taps that landed in the gap were lost (one, at 126 ms, reopened the menu). The gap is not resolved by the approaches tested. One approach was tried, keeping the window longer: it only moved the gap later, so it was reverted. The evidence does not establish that no application-level approach could address it.
  • Only the delays in the table were tried. The check reports the gap cases as notes, not failures. Its "all checks passed" therefore doesn't mean every reopen timing works.

Correction to the earlier motion review

CorrectedThe motion review's Android "observation" of this defect came from a faulty test. Its script sent Back and the tap as one sh -c "…" command through a helper that splits arguments, so the device ran only sh -c input and Back was never sent.

The defect itself is real. It was reproduced afterwards with Back and the tap sent as one device command, and the gap read from the device's input log, as in the table above. The review is kept as written, with a dated correction note beside the finding.
